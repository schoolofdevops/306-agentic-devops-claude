Orchestration Topologies Without Chaos — Quiz

An incident opens: orders-api is erroring, and three independent causes are possible (application/retry, platform/resource, recent-change). You want all three investigated at once while you keep steering the incident. Which topology fits?

Your teammate says the cost of picking the wrong orchestration shape is a syntax error you catch right away. Why is that framing dangerous during an incident?

The Ops Lead pattern is described as an interactive parent that delegates and synthesizes. What is the one thing it must never do?

The Ops Lead uses sequential delegation for an incident: investigate first, then review a proposed fix. What makes sequential the right shape here rather than a parallel fan-out?

You fan out three investigators, but each is told to read the previous investigator's conclusion first “for context.” They all return the same root cause. Why is this NOT strong confirmation?

Two of three blinded investigators say “retry storm” and one says “bad deploy.” The synthesizer refuses to declare “retry storm wins 2–1.” What should it do instead?

You fan out an estate audit “across the whole estate” without dividing the work. Two workers both audit the networking module and nobody audits storage. Which anti-pattern is this, and what is the control?

The Ops Lead delegates: “Confirm the retry storm is causing the incident.” Why is this delegation defective, and how do you fix it?

The estate audit must gate a merge in CI, so it runs as a workflow. It produces the identical answer at --max-parallel 1, 2, and 4. Why does that property matter?

The lesson contrasts a “detective interview” with an “assembly-line inspection.” Which job maps to the assembly-line inspection, and why?
